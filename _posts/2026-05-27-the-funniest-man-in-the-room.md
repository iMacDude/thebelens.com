Mac was my mentor.

I didn't know it when I met him. That's how the best mentors work — they don't announce themselves. They just show up, do what they do, and somewhere along the way you realize the person sitting across from you has been shaping you the whole time.

Guy "Mac" McGee was an old parts guy. I was a second-shift technician and service writer, young and still figuring out which way was up. Around 9pm, Mac would drift off to sleep at the parts counter, an unfiltered Pall Mall slowly extinguishing itself in its ashtray beside him. So knowledgeable. So unapologetically, completely, entirely *himself*.

That was the thing about Mac. He didn't perform. He didn't manage impressions. He just *was* — and once you were lucky enough to get past the outer crust and earn a little of his trust, what you found inside was one of the warmest, funniest, most genuinely wise human beings you were ever going to meet.

---

When he finally got used to me, he leaned over one day and asked if I had a roll of nickels.

*"Friday night. We play two-dollar poverty poker. At my place."*

And that was it. That was the invitation. A roll of nickels and a Friday night that turned into a tear-jerking, cheek-hurting, two-to-three-hour sober blast — winner take all, everybody laughing so hard there were actual tears, and then a sleepy drive home to start the weekend, already looking forward to Monday because Monday meant Mac was back at the parts counter.

More than once, Mac and I would be doubled over laughing, tears streaming down our faces, when company leaders would cross the threshold into the parts department, take one look at the two of us, verbalize a single word — *"Nope."* — and walk directly back the other direction. They knew better than to get pulled into the comical vortex. Nobody came back out the same.

---

Mac bought a house. Which meant Mac needed a lawn mower.

He grabbed his Home Depot ad and headed to the store. Found the mower he wanted. Stood next to the floor model for what seemed like hours, studying it the way only Mac could study something, until a clerk finally wandered over and asked if he needed help.

Mac said he wanted to buy the lawn mower — pointed at the ad.

The clerk told him they were out of that model.

Mac looked at the floor model. Looked at the clerk. Looked at the floor model again.

*"You mean THIS model?"*

Yes, said the clerk. That one.

Mac, genuinely perplexed, said — *well, can I buy this one?*

And so began a long walk around the store, Mac pushing his soon-to-be prize through the aisles toward the checkout. As he approached the registers, he accidentally clipped the heel of the gentleman in front of him. The man spun around with an expression that was not particularly friendly.

Mac, quick as a flash, pointed at the mower and said:

*"Hey Buddy!! You seen any GRASS?!"*

The man's eyes went wide. He turned back around. He never made eye contact again.

Mac brought his new mower home, read the directions carefully, and — wanting to test it before taking it outside — made the small error of plugging it in indoors.

It burned a perfectly circular 23-inch ring into his shag carpet.

Nothing, Mac reasoned, that a throw rug couldn't fix.

---

Mac wasn't pure comedy. That's the thing people might miss if I told just the funny stories. He laced his comedic life with sage wisdom — the kind that lands harder precisely because it comes wrapped in a laugh. He was brutally honest in the way only people who genuinely care about you can be. He had integrity down to the bone. And he had a zest for life that I have never seen matched, before or since.

He became one of the most influential men of my young career. Not because he sat me down and taught me lessons. But because he *lived* in front of me — fully, authentically, hilariously — and showed me what it looked like to be completely at peace with who you are.

I am so grateful for his friendship. For his honesty. For every Friday night poker game and every tear-streaked laugh and every time he fell asleep at that parts counter with his Pall Mall burning down beside him like a slow, contented fuse.

Rest in peace, Guy "Mac" McGee.

You will always, always be missed.
