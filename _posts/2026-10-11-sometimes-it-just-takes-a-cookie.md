I chatted with my son, yesterday, and we had a good laugh about a great memory.

Many years ago, when he was very small, we went to visit one of my dearest friends. You know the kind that you feel more like brothers and though long gaps in time exist between speaking, any conversation that happens is like it was only yesterday that we were together.

My friend was a firefighter, and an amazing role model. He had tried on several occasions to convince me to consider that path. A noble career that I was close to pursuing, though it was usually the thought of handling burn victims that I could never overcome. You see, my mother was a pediatric nurse, and I attended Parkwood-Upjohn school, which was not only an elementary school, but a rehabilitation school. I used to visit some of her former patients and send her love. They were kids of my own age, at the time, and I would grow to have a much greater respect for her work, and their brave recoveries, over the following years.

Though I don’t remember the time of year, or the occasion, I took my son to go visit the firehouse. He was maybe 4 or 5? It was a long time ago, lol. We visited the firehouse, saw the fire trucks and had a great visit. We wandered outside and Tim began to play on the bench, while the young adults passed the time. Though my friend had already been a fireman for a few years, I was the first of the 2 of us to have kids of my own, and enjoyed that challenge, probably as much as he enjoyed being a fireman.

While we were chatting, something happened, and Tim did a nose dive off the bench and into the unforgiving concrete sidewalk. There was momentary panic as we watched the delayed action in his eyes, and the tears beginning to well. I’ve always tried to be the one to avoid extreme reactions, but my dad-moment, and already lengthy experience in my son’s “in his father’s footsteps” calamity (We didn’t nickname him The Timinator, for no reason), triggered what would become a great teaching moment.

I reached into my jacket pocket, pulled out the pack of “Famous Amos” cookies, and quickly offered him a deferring alternative to a blood curdling scream…

I’m not sure whether the expression of shock in my friend’s eyes at the time Tim did the header, or my friend’s reaction to the response from the cookie was greater. I just know the result.

With one cookie in hand, and the other in his mouth, I crouched down and made a remark that will later become the Hallmark of my often unsuccessful parenting. “You ok? Awesome! Hop up, brush yourself off, and let’s keep going!”

In the book of Proverbs 24:16 it says, “for though they fall seven times, they will rise again...”

I’m the youngest of seven, calamity of one kind or another has been a part of life, and without the love of humor, instilled in me from my mother, and amazing family, I would have gone even more mad 🤪.

My son experienced this same thing yesterday, as his fearless daughter played out a similar scene on a slide. It’s the sharing of those moments, that make having kids and grandkids so special.

Kids are amazing. Truly, amazing. They are the miracles that we have to love, cherish, and raise, bravely. Sadly, we can’t always be there to keep them from scraping their knees and frankly, we shouldn’t. It’s often our greatest achievements that are inspired or elicited from our greatest struggles, or our biggest bumps and bruises. We live in a world surrounded by calamity, some very good, and some very bad. Teaching our children problem-solving skills, conflict resolution, and how to navigate the good and the bad, in life, is no easy task. There’s no manual. Learning to LOVE in a world that often seems unloveable, seems to me more of an art form than anything else. Understanding the journey is what’s important, and not the destination. But isn’t love itself, an art? I suppose perception is the thing we need to teach?

Pope Francis will tell us, in his 2017 Ted Talk, “We all need each other.”

The experiences and the principles are not so different in parenting as they are in leadership and mentoring. So often, as I sit down to write or talk about life’s influences and experiences, it’s easy to draw the similarities between family and work.

I think being “genuine” should be a part of who we are, and not just present in either home or work lives. Why should they be different?

We live in a world that tries to separate us. The news, our phones, Covid, and so much more. We, as a people NEED connection, and love. The kind of connection where friendship spans decades, where our neighbors and yes our customers, just need to be heard. Where sometimes, a simple cookie, a brush off of the dust, helps us hop up, and let’s keep going!
