It's happened more times than I can count.

A little calamity unfolds — something spills, something breaks, something goes sideways in the particular way that things go sideways in bars and restaurants and beach shacks and all the places Kelley and I love to be — and the person responsible looks at us with genuine relief in their eyes and says it:

*"I'm so glad it was you guys."*

---

We got to our favorite beach bar right when they opened on a recent morning. They were still setting up, still cleaning, still in that gentle pre-open chaos that the best places have — the kind that tells you the staff actually cares how things look before the day starts.

And then a hose let loose.

Water, everywhere. Not a disaster. Not a crisis. Just one of those perfect little moments of physics and bad timing. Our server got it under control fast, and as she did, she looked up at us with wide eyes and said exactly that:

*"Oh my God. I'm so glad it was you guys, and not anyone else."*

And we laughed. All three of us. Because what else do you do?

---

I've thought about why this keeps happening. Why we seem to find ourselves, again and again, on the receiving end of spilled drinks and wayward hoses and orders that went to the wrong table and all the other small human errors that populate a life lived out in the world among people.

Part of it is simple math — Kelley and I go out a lot. We love it. We love the people, we love the ritual of it, we love sitting somewhere with a good drink and watching the world move around us. If you're somewhere often enough, you're going to be there when things go wrong.

But I don't think that's all of it.

I think it's that we both know — deeply, practically, from a lifetime of evidence — how fragile and error-prone the whole human enterprise actually is. We've both made mistakes. We've both had the thing go wrong on our watch. We've both been the person standing there hoping that whoever's on the other end of our error is going to be kind about it.

And so when it's our turn to be on that other end, it doesn't feel like a grievance. It feels like Tuesday. It feels like *of course this happened, things happen, let's laugh about it and move on.*

---

What Kelley and I are not — and I think this matters — is uptight.

We are not entitled. We are not pretentious. We don't arrive anywhere with an expectation bar set so high that reality can only disappoint us. We arrive because we genuinely want to be there. We stay because we love the people. We come back because something about the place — or more often, the person — made us feel welcome.

And the beautiful thing that's happened, over years of showing up this way, is that the people have started to feel it.

Our favorite servers know our names. They light up a little when we walk in — not in a performed hospitality way, but in the way a person lights up when they see someone they actually like. We've built something in those places. Not a transaction, not a loyalty program, not an accumulation of points. Something more like friendship.

You don't get that by being the table that sends things back twice and leaves a pointed note on the receipt.

---

There's a principle underneath all of this that I keep coming back to. I've believed it my whole career, in every dealership and every meeting room and every parts counter I've ever stood behind:

*Take care of the people, and the rest follows.*

Not the experience. Not the optics. The people.

When you genuinely love the people you're with — not what they can do for you, not how well they perform, but the actual human beings — something shifts. They can feel it. And they give it back in ways you never asked for.

Like relieved laughter over a runaway hose.

Like *"I'm so glad it was you guys."*

---

Kelley and I are far from perfect. We're late sometimes. We forget things. We have our moments of impatience and frustration like every other couple that spends real time together. But when we walk into the places we love, we leave all of that outside.

We're there because we want to be there. We love the people we're with. We know that things will sometimes go sideways.

And when they do — we're glad we're the ones sitting in that seat.

*The zest for life that makes all of this possible? That's another story entirely.*
