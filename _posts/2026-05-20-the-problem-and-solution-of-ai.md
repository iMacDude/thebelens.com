Let me be clear about something right upfront: I love AI. Not cautiously. Not with reservations. Not with one eye on the exit. I use it every single day, and it has fundamentally changed the way I think, work, and create.

That's not the problem.

The problem is what most people are doing with it.

---

## The Most Powerful Tool I've Ever Touched

I've been in technology for over thirty years. I've watched industries transform around software that nobody understood until it was too late to ignore. I've seen tools come and go — some that changed everything, most that changed nothing.

AI is different. Not because of what it *does*, but because of what it *demands* from you.

I don't use AI as a worker bee. I don't set it to task like a vending machine — insert prompt, receive output, move on. That's the laziest possible use of the most powerful thinking tool ever built, and it's what I see most people doing.

What I *actually* do is require AI to think. And more importantly — I require it to make *me* think. I push back on it. I challenge it. I ask it to challenge me. I use it to stress-test ideas I've been carrying around for years and to build things I could only half-imagine on my own.

For me, AI is an expressive release of creational architecture. A partner in my long-term thought strategy. An inspirational force that takes what I already know and helps me see it from angles I never would have found alone.

Honestly? Where has this been for the last forty years.

---

## The Hard Question Nobody Wants to Ask

Here's where we get uncomfortable. And I say this with genuine love for the people who need to hear it:

*If AI does everything for you — then what good are you?*

Think about that slowly. If AI writes your emails, builds your presentations, answers your questions, generates your strategies, and produces your work product — what exactly are you contributing? What is it that your company is paying for when they pay for you?

Because AI is getting cheaper every day. Faster every day. More capable every day. If your value to your organization lives entirely in tasks that AI can perform — you are in a genuinely precarious position, and I'd rather tell you that now than let you find out later.

This isn't a warning. It's an invitation.

---

## What AI Actually Cannot Do

Here's what I know from daily, serious, professional use of AI:

It does not have my forty years of walking dealership floors and knowing what a service lane *feels* like when it's running well versus when it's about to fall apart. It cannot read a room. It cannot hear the thing a client isn't saying. It does not know which rule to break and when, because it's learned — from experience, not from data — that sometimes the right answer isn't in the manual.

AI does not have my cumulative knowledge, my specific expertise, or the particular way my brain connects things that most people never connect. It doesn't have yours either.

And here's what I've realized, more clearly now than ever before in my career: I am *more* grateful for my gifts today than I have ever been — precisely *because* AI exists. Because now I can leverage those gifts in ways I never could alone. AI didn't make me less valuable. It revealed exactly how valuable the things that make me *me* actually are.

The people who will thrive in an AI-driven world are not the ones who use AI to avoid thinking. They are the ones who use AI to think *bigger* than they ever could before.

---

## AI Is Here. Now What?

AI is not leaving. It is not slowing down. It is not going to politely step aside while the workforce figures out how to feel about it.

So here's my challenge — especially to the younger people reading this who are still forming their relationship with these tools:

**Don't be lazy.**

Be proactive. Be curious. Be the person in the room who asks AI the hard questions and then pushes back on the answers. Bring your judgment, your integrity, your lived experience, your perspective — the things that took you years to build and that no model can replicate overnight.

Use AI to leverage what makes you extraordinary. Not to replace the work of becoming extraordinary in the first place.

The partnership between human intelligence and artificial intelligence — when it's done right — is one of the most exciting things I've witnessed in a long career of watching technology change the world.

I'm not afraid of it. I'm grateful for it. And I'm not done building with it.

Neither should you be.
